# Render the model first, let AI finish

Nobody credible makes a photoreal fly-through of an unbuilt building straight from a prompt. Top studios build the model in 3ds Max with V-Ray or Corona, or in Unreal, dress it by hand, render it, and only then use AI. They use it for motion on their own CGI plates, for upscaling, and to upgrade people and plants. The viral Instagram reels look better than they are accurate: a Midjourney or Nano Banana still, a Magnific upscale and a 5 to 10 second Kling or Veo push, with no tie to a real design. Our pipeline already does the hard, rare part, which is pulling exact geometry out of IFC and proving it hasn't drifted. Our 4.5/10 hero still doesn't come from Blender or Cycles. It comes from IFC solids that nobody re-dressed: box-projected textures on unbevelled boxes, joinery with no reveals, a floor that's one tiled surface, a camera parked square-on, too little bounce light and too few props. Each of those faults has a known fix, and most of the fixes can be scripted. On top of them, AI earns a fixed, gated job for stills: a low-strength ComfyUI pass driven by Blender's own exact depth and line passes. The tower-to-unit-to-room move should stay a set of Cycles shots joined in the scroll, with AI video kept for social clips. Done in the order in the plan at the end, the automated steps should lift the hero to about 6.5 to 7 for under A$400 in tools. The styling kit, the AI pass and one to two hours of human art direction per hero shot should take it to about 8.

All AUD figures assume about A$1.53 per US$1 and are rounded. Check vendor pages before buying. The score gains are our judgement from the review faults, not measured results.

## Professionals render the model; Instagram creators prompt the picture

The studio tier hasn't changed its core tools. Binyan Studios, with offices in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, London, New York and Los Angeles, builds its pipeline on 3ds Max ([Autodesk AREA](https://area.autodesk.com/life-in-3d/from-sketch-to-render-using-3ds-max-binyan-studios/)). The Boundary used Corona as the only pre-rendering engine for a full London marketing suite ([Chaos](https://www.chaos.com/architectural-visualization)). An Australian studio describes the standard flow as CAD or BIM in, finish-schedule mapping, clay proofs, then rendering and grading in V-Ray, Corona or Unreal Engine 5 at 4K or 8K ([Elipse Studio](https://elipsestudio.com/blog/architectural-visualisation-for-australian-property-developers)). The CGarchitect 2024-25 survey still shows V-Ray and Corona dominating final-quality work, with Unreal, Lumion and Vantage gaining, and **56% of respondents already using AI tools somewhere in the workflow** ([CGconnect survey](https://cgconnect.chaos.com/features/articles/2120b451-2024-25-rendering-engine-survey-results?from=cgarchitect)). Chaos now owns that survey host, so read it as vendor-adjacent.

Where studios use AI, they put it on top of their own renders. KPF's applied technology team animates its own static renders in Runway for pitches, where outsourced animation used to take about two weeks and "thousands of dollars" per minute ([Runway, KPF](https://runway.com/customers/how-kpf-is-incorporating-ai-video-into-architectural-workflows)). A Binyan senior artist describes AI video that starts from CGI base scenes, with the team directing camera, shots and transitions ([LinkedIn profile snippet](https://www.linkedin.com/in/louise-richardson/)). That's a weak source, but it's the clearest sign yet of the hybrid at a top Australian studio. Chaos built its AI Enhancer specifically to make flat CG people and plants photoreal inside Corona and V-Ray ([Digital Engineering](https://www.digitalengineering247.com/article/chaos-debuts-several-ai-tools)). Even studios with 15,000-asset libraries still struggle with entourage. An Australian studio warns that AI photo-to-video fails for off-the-plan work. With nothing real to photograph, it produces bent door frames, drifting furniture, wrong reflections and spec drift between runs ([Maverick Frame](https://maverickframe.com/blog/ai-video-for-real-estate/)).

Instagram-scale work runs the other way round. Studio Tim Fu's "Living Sketches" are Midjourney, Photoshop and Kling, with atmosphere over accuracy ([Architizer](https://architizer.com/blog/?p=203116)). Paid courses teach Midjourney 7, Nano Banana Pro edits and Luma or Kling keyframe animation, often running each still through Magnific before Kling Pro so the detail carries into motion ([PAACADEMY](https://paacademy.com/course/cinematic-architecture-ai-powered-design-workflows); [MyArchitectAI course list](https://www.myarchitectai.com/blog/ai-architecture-courses)). We couldn't fetch the reels themselves, so individual creators' settings stay unverified. The pattern is consistent, though. Dramatic golden-hour light, mist and water carry the look, and the camera moves slowly so the model has little to break. That recipe can't sell a real apartment, because nothing ties the window count to the drawings.

The price ladder shows where an automated pipeline fits. In Australia a marketing hero exterior costs **A$1,500 to A$6,000+**, an interior still **A$800 to A$5,000** and a 60 second fly-through **A$4,000 to A$25,000+**. A single still takes a few days and an animation four to six weeks ([Feasly](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/architectural-rendering-australia-developer-guide)). Full off-the-plan campaigns run **A$14,000 to A$28,000** ([Elipse Studio](https://elipsestudio.com/blog/architectural-visualisation-for-australian-property-developers)). Studio artists log roughly 12 to 20 hours per hero image, mostly on modelling, styling, materials and revisions rather than rendering ([CGArchitect forum](https://forums.cgarchitect.com/topic/79516-how-long-time-do-you-get); [CadCrowd](https://www.cadcrowd.com/blog/?p=50813)). Those are the hours our pipeline currently skips, and they're the hours the score is missing.

## Our 4.5 comes from IFC geometry nobody re-dressed

The reviewer's faults all trace back to one place. Studios almost never feed raw IFC to a renderer. They push Revit or ArchiCAD through live-sync, or export to 3ds Max, and then **re-materialise and dress the model by hand** ([Epic, Datasmith IFC](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/importing-ifc-files-into-unreal-engine-using-datasmith?lang=en-US); [Epic, Twinmotion import](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/twinmotion/import-process-in-twinmotion)). Epic's own advice for IFC is to replace the default materials, because IFC carries almost no surface information. Our pipeline automates the import and then skips the dressing. The table maps each fault to its cause and to a fix that can be scripted.

| Reviewer fault | Root cause | Pro fix | Script it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone pattern scale | Procedural marble, world box projection | Real slab photo (about 3.2 x 1.6 m) mapped once at true size on a planar UV, veining running round the edge | Yes; slab choice is human |
| Tiled-looking floor | One slab face with a repeating texture | Floorboards as real geometry with per-board texture and tint offset and micro bevels | Yes |
| Flat joinery | IFC cabinets are single boxes | Separate door and drawer panels, 1 to 2 mm shadow gaps, edge banding, handles, kick recess | Partly |
| Weak AO | Nothing to occlude: no crevices or gaps | Contact shadows come from geometry detail, not an AO pass | Yes, as a result of the joinery and floor fixes |
| Grey far side | Exposure set for the windows, too few bounces | Raise interior exposure, 8 to 12+ bounces, a light-linked fill on the darkest large surface | Yes |
| Camera too far back, square-on | Scripted camera from room bounds | 1.1 to 1.4 m height, 20 to 35 degrees off a corner, 24 to 28 mm hero, Shift Y not tilt, plus 85 to 100 mm detail crops | Candidates yes; choosing partly |
| Sparse styling | About a dozen CC0 props | 40 to 150 curated, correctly scaled items per kitchen hero | Placement partly; curation human |

The materials and joinery fixes now have off-the-shelf parts. NodeCrafted Floor Generator 3.0 is a Geometry Nodes add-on that turns any surface into boards or tiles, including herringbone and chevron. It mixes up to 16 textures randomly across the boards and costs **US$24 (about A$37)** ([Superhive](https://superhivemarket.com/products/floor-generator?num=3&src=top-rated)). Blender 5.0 added shader closures, which make "blended box mapping, tiling and texture bombing" possible with any procedural texture. That gives us one node group for anti-tiling on every large surface, with no add-on needed ([Blender 5.0 notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.0/rendering/)). Cycles light linking, added in Blender 4.0, lets a fill light reach only the far cabinets without flattening the rest of the room ([Blender 4.0 Cycles notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/4.0/cycles/)). For the camera, the useful rules are a level camera with Shift for straight verticals, a two-point view off a corner, eye height around 1.55 m and moderate lenses ([Columbia technical camera](https://smorgasbord.cdp.arch.columbia.edu/modules/40-rendering-with-blender/252-technical-camera); [Studio Matrx](https://www.studiomatrx.org/students/real-time-vr-for-architecture/cameras-and-composition); [D5 camera tips](https://www.d5render.com/posts/camera-setting-tips-must-known-for-archviz)). No pro survey backs the exact height range, so treat it as course guidance.

Styling is still where most of the effort goes. The free libraries are now big enough to supply bulk props. BlenderKit offers tens of thousands of free models and materials for commercial work ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/09/get-over-48000-free-3d-models-materials-and-hdris-from-blenderkit/)), and Epic made the full Megascans library free on Fab under a licence covering any DCC tool ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=179945)). Those are news reports; check the licence text itself before relying on them. The constraint isn't licensing. It's curation: every asset needs consistent scale, origin, real-world UVs and tags so a script can place it. The closest public model for automatic placement is **Infinigen Indoors**, a procedural, headless, Blender-based system with constraint rules for clutter on surfaces ([arXiv 2406.11824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11824)). Its constraint language is worth reading before we write our own placer. AI 3D generators (Hunyuan3D, TRELLIS.2, Tripo, Meshy, Rodin) are good enough for background ceramics and books but not for hero furniture. Every comparison we found was vendor-run ([3D AI Studio](https://3daistudio.com/state-of-ai-3d-generation-2026)).

## AI earns a defined slot in stills, not a free hand

There are two working routes for turning a render into a photoreal still while keeping the geometry. The more controllable one is ComfyUI with Flux or SD, run as img2img with structure control. The control is a depth pass plus a soft-edge or lineart pass taken from your own render. For a global polish on a finished render, **denoise stays at 0.2 to 0.35**. Facade-only passes run at 0.15 to 0.25. A masked foreground (plants, wet paving, sky) can go to 0.4 and above. Unmasked 0.6 is where facades get redrawn ([ArchiGen AI](https://archigenai.com/ai-render-enhance-without-hallucination-denoise-controlnet-architects-2026.html)). The same source says lineart at full weight over the whole frame pulls materials back toward the flat original, so control should stay firm on the building and ease off over the foreground. It's a workflow essay rather than a test log, and the usable band moves with each model, so fix the seed and the prompt per project ([ArchiGen AI](https://archigenai.com/comfyui-archviz-question-nobody-answers-2026.html)).

That's a real advantage for us. Creators who only have a flat image estimate depth with tools like Depth Anything 3, and thin elements such as railings and mullions then vanish or grow halos that ControlNet reads as edges ([ArchiGen AI](https://archigenai.com/depth-anything-v3-flat-render-control-comfyui-architects-2026.html)). We render exact depth, normal, line and mask passes from the model, so we skip that whole failure class.

The second route is an instruction-edit model, Nano Banana Pro (Gemini 3 Pro Image), which also runs inside Chaos Veras 4.0. The best documented recipe changes one thing in our pipeline. **Feed the model a colour and material pass with AO, not a clay render.** From clay, the model "doesn't know if the white floor is resin, carpet, or bleached wood". Pair it with a keep-clause about framing, perspective and materials, and make the short edge at least 1440 px ([CGwisdom](https://cgwisdom.com/blog/sketchup-and-nano-banana-pro-photorealistic-visualizations-in-5-minutes-is-this-the-end-of-v-ray.html)). Mirrors fail unless the prompt describes what they reflect. User reports on Veras are split: one says it respects the Revit geometry, another says it "takes too much creative liberty" even with the geometry override set low ([aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/veras-review/)). For a buyer-facing image, that makes Nano Banana a mood and entourage tool, not a finishing tool. A GPL add-on, Nanode, already sends Blender depth and mist passes to Nano Banana from inside Blender ([GitHub](https://github.com/Kovname/nano-banana-render)).

For upscaling, Magnific's Creativity setting invents detail and its Resemblance setting pulls back toward the source. It also has a Precision mode that enlarges without inventing ([Magnific docs](https://www.magnific.com/de/ai/docs/image-upscaler)). Its pricing conflicts between US$39 and US$99 a month ([MyArchitectAI](https://www.myarchitectai.com/blog/magnific-ai-pricing)). The compute for a hero still is small: five to ten generations plus one upscale comes in under about US$5. The failure modes are well known: windows added or moved, mullions changed, corners rounded, a bay dropped every few runs, shadows the scene couldn't cast. The published QA is a manual window count bay by bay ([ArchiGen AI](https://archigenai.com/ai-render-enhance-without-hallucination-denoise-controlnet-architects-2026.html)). Our edge-map drift check automates part of that. The AI pass must stay behind it, plus a per-material colour check.

## The tower-to-room move is cut, not generated

No engine or AI tool offers a continuous "tower exterior into the unit into the room" move as a feature. Studios cut between separate camera shots or use section planes in a sequencer. The AI-video version chains start-and-end-frame clips. In Kling 2.5 Turbo, 3.0 or O1, or in Veo 3.1, the start frame sets composition and light and the end frame sets where the shot lands. The prompt describes the movement, not the scene, and the last frame of one clip becomes the first of the next ([Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/A-Guide-to-Kling-Turbo-Start-End-Frame); [AI FILMS Studio](https://studio.aifilms.ai/blog/kling-3-o1-video-generation-tutorial)). Keep the two frames close. When they differ too much, a seam appears, because the end frame is a target rather than a stitched-in image ([imagetovideo.dev](https://imagetovideo.dev/models/kling/)). The one ten-model comparison we found, from a vendor, tested fire, water and crowds, not straight facades. **Nobody has published a benchmark of how well these models hold architectural geometry** ([Pulze](https://www.pulze.io/blog/ai-video-model-comparison)).

Cost isn't the obstacle. At API rates, and assuming four tries per usable second, a 60 second film costs about **US$17 (A$26) on Kling 2.6** and about **US$96 (A$147) on Veo 3.1 Standard** at 1080p ([fal Kling 2.6](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/kling-video/v2.6/pro/image-to-video); [benchlm Veo pricing](https://benchlm.ai/media-pricing/veo)). The obstacle is accuracy mid-clip. So the scroll site keeps Cycles frame sequences, which are exact and flicker-free. AI video goes only into short social clips that connect two of our own Cycles keyframes, checked frame by frame, and never into window views. A more locked-down variant restyles a cheap EEVEE animation with Wan VACE using depth control, which holds 3D structure better than edges ([Comfy hub](https://comfy.org/hub/workflows/video-restyle-5931e9bdf9db/); [VACE paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.07598)). World models such as Genie 3 are 60-second research previews whose output "may not... adhere closely to prompts, images" ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/project-genie/)). They're useful for surrounding context at most.

Gaussian splats are the one genuinely new option for the web, and they reverse an earlier conclusion. Training a splat **from our own renders** rather than from photos is now documented. Camera poses are exact, so the structure-from-motion step goes away. One test rendered 120 to 200 views headlessly and trained in about 22 seconds on an RTX 3060 ([RadianceFields](https://radiancefields.com/training-gaussian-splats-from-renders-not-photos)). The Splatlight add-on does this from Blender 4.2 to 5.2 with a free tier ([RadianceFields](https://radiancefields.com/splatlight-generates-gaussian-splats-from-blender-scenes-without-a-colmap-solve)). World Labs' `posedImages2Splats` API takes exactly what a Blender job emits (an image, a camera and a depth EXR per view) for **US$5 per refined run**. It names skies, plain walls, windows and thin structures as weak cases ([RadianceFields](https://radiancefields.com/world-labs-opens-gaussian-splat-creation-in-marble)). Because the splat bakes our Cycles look, it's as accurate as the render it came from. That makes it suitable for an unbuilt interior or a cutaway model as an optional "explore" moment, delivered with Spark on three.js or PlayCanvas streamed SOG ([Spark](https://github.com/sparkjsdev/spark); [PlayCanvas](https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/supersplat/streaming/)). A glazed tower exterior is still a poor fit.

Construction stages get simpler too. IFC classes (footing, column, slab, wall, curtain wall, roof, covering) crossed with `IfcBuildingStorey` give a natural "frame to slab to envelope to fit-out" order with **no programme file**. Bonsai's own IfcTask 4D tool is described by its developer as "pretty basic" ([OSArch](https://community.osarch.org/discussion/comment/9871/)). Twinmotion's phasing gives prettier output than Navisworks but isn't schedule-driven ([IMAGINiT](https://resources.imaginit.com/twinmotion-tips-and-tricks/4d-timeline-simulation-navisworks-versus-twinmotion)). The risk is data quality: elements exported as `IfcBuildingElementProxy` break the grouping, so the script needs a fallback mapping by name or layer.

## What this changes in the earlier research

The earlier showcase-process note got the main diagnosis right: the gap is taste and dressing, not the renderer. Its GPU, styling-kit, sheer, slab-photo, imperfection and post-production steps all stand. This research confirms that switching engines doesn't help. Twinmotion doesn't list IFC as an import format and, like D5, Lumion and Enscape, has no documented scripting. Unreal is free below US$1M revenue and scriptable through Movie Render Queue, but it's a far heavier toolchain with long-sequence memory crashes reported in 5.6 ([Epic MRQ](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-command-line-rendering-with-move-render-queue-in-unreal-engine); [UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/movie-render-queue-long-sequence-memory-leak-crashes-after-2-3-hours-ue5-6-1080p-64gb-ram/2636773)). Where this report changes or extends that note:

| Earlier conclusion | Change in this report |
|---|---|
| Gap is mainly styling and taste | Extended: a named mechanical root cause (box-projected, un-UV'd, unbevelled IFC solids) explains stone, floor, joinery and AO faults, and most of it is scriptable |
| Taste can't be automated | Narrowed: camera candidates can be generated and scored by rules, with a person or vision model picking from 10 to 20. Human time per hero drops but doesn't go away |
| AI polish is an optional last 10% | Upgraded to a defined step for stills: ComfyUI with our exact depth and line passes at 0.2 to 0.35 denoise, masked foreground at 0.4+, and Nano Banana fed a material pass, not clay. Still gated by the drift and finishes checks |
| Splats never for unbuilt interiors | Reversed for splats trained from our own Cycles renders; still never from invented content |
| 4D needs a programme CSV | Extended: an IFC class-by-storey build-up needs no schedule; a programme adds date accuracy |
| Outsourced hero still about A$230 to A$1,240 | Australian studio pricing is higher: A$800 to A$5,000 per interior and A$1,500 to A$6,000+ per exterior hero |
| AI law: a 2025 NSW bill | NSW passed AI-image disclosure for rental ads in Sept 2026, up to A$22,000, in force early 2027; sales renders still sit under s18 ACL and *Ripani* ([SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/property/realty-check-nsw-demands-disclosures-for-ai-altered-property-photos/)). Sources still conflict on the *Ripani* appeal outcome |

## Prioritised action plan

The order is quality gained per dollar and hour, with fully automatable steps first. GPU hire is assumed from the earlier note at about A$0.55 to A$1.15 per hour for an RTX 4090 ([Findstack](https://findstack.com/resources/runpod-vs-vast-ai)). Without it, none of the iteration below is practical on a 16 GB laptop.

| # | Step | Cost (AUD) | Automated? | Expected gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rent a GPU for finals and iteration (from the earlier note) | about A$1 per hour; a few dollars per hero | Yes | Enables every step below |
| 2 | Camera generator: 1.1 to 1.4 m, 20 to 35 degrees off a corner, 24 to 28 mm, Shift Y, plus detail crops; render 10 to 20 candidates and pick | A$0 | Candidates yes, pick partly | +0.5 to 1.0 |
| 3 | Exposure and bounce fix: auto-exposure from a test render, 8 to 12 bounces, a light-linked fill on the darkest large surface | A$0 | Yes | +0.5 |
| 4 | Real-world UVs at import; supplier slab photo mapped at true size on benchtops | A$0 (slab photos free on request) | Yes | +0.5 |
| 5 | Floorboards as geometry with Floor Generator 3.0 | about A$37 one-off | Yes | +0.5 |
| 6 | Joinery skinner in Geometry Nodes: panels, 1 to 2 mm reveals, bevels, edge banding, handles, kick recess (fixes AO as a side effect) | A$0 plus build time | Partly | +0.5 to 1.0 |
| 7 | Anti-tiling closure group, imperfection layer and a saved compositor preset (light groups, Cryptomatte, mild glare, vignette) | A$0 | Yes | +0.5 |
| 8 | Styling kit of 50 to 150 tagged items per room type, placed by Infinigen-style rules; sheers on every window | BlenderKit about A$14 a month, Evermotion volume A$45 to A$230, curtain pack about A$60, Botaniq about A$200 for exteriors | Placement partly, curation no | +1.0 to 1.5 |
| 9 | AI still pass on the GPU: ComfyUI, Blender depth and lineart, denoise 0.2 to 0.35, masked foreground 0.4+, then Magnific Precision; must pass the drift and finishes checks | under A$8 compute per hero; Magnific about A$60 to A$150 a month (optional) | Yes, behind the QA gate | +0.5 to 1.0 |
| 10 | Human art-direction pass against a reference, one to two hours per hero | labour only | No | +0.5 to 1.0 |
| 11 | Automatic build-up from IFC class by storey, rendered as a frame sequence | A$0 | Yes | New deliverable for builders |
| 12 | Splat test of one interior from a 150 to 300 view Cycles orbit, shown in Spark | A$0 (Splatlight Lite) or about A$8 per Marble run | Yes | New "explore" moment |
| 13 | Social clips only: Kling or Veo start-and-end chaining between our own Cycles keyframes, checked frame by frame | about A$26 to A$147 per finished minute | Partly | Reach, not site quality |

Steps 2 to 7 together cost under A$50 in tools plus about two weeks of scripting. Adding them to the 4.5 still should land it around 6.5 to 7. Step 8 is the largest single gain and the biggest one-off effort, taking it to about 7.5. Steps 9 and 10 are how studios get the last point, and should bring a hero to about 8. These are estimates. Re-score the same kitchen after steps 2 to 7 and again after step 10 before promising any client a number. If one hero is needed faster than this, buying it from a studio for A$800 to A$5,000 is the honest fallback. Don't switch engines, don't use prompt-first generators for buyer-facing images, and don't pay for pixel streaming.

## Conclusion

The useful shift is seeing that "professional quality" is two separate jobs, and that the market prices them very differently. Studios charge thousands for both together because, for them, the dressing and the accuracy are the same hand labour. We already automate the accuracy, and the research shows the dressing splits into a large mechanical part (UVs, boards, reveals, exposure, camera candidates) and a small judgement part (styling choices, the final pick, the grade). Script the mechanical part, use AI only inside the bounds our own passes set, and the human hours per hero fall from a studio's 12 to 20 to one or two. That's the margin that lets a A$3,000 to A$10,000 model-to-scroll-site package exist for townhouse and boutique projects that studios price out.

The second implication is commercial. Every regulator and competitor warning points at the same risk: an image that drifts from the approved plan. Our drift and finishes checks, plus a per-frame record of the plan revision, are the one thing neither the Instagram creators nor most studios can show. They should be marketed as the product, with the photorealism as the price of entry. The open questions are still empirical: how Kling and Veo hold straight facades mid-clip, how splats handle glazing, and whether the score really moves as predicted. The next blind test answers the last one cheaply.
