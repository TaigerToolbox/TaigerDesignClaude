# Off-the-plan property marketing visuals in Australia (2025-2026): market, production, pricing, AI, legal, and the gap

Research date: 16 Nov 2026. About 20 searches and fetches. Many price sources are vendor-written (studio blogs, "cost guides"), so treat the figures as indicative. Where I relied on general knowledge without a source found this session, I put it under Gaps, not Findings.

## Who are the leading studios, and what is their production workflow and toolset?

### Takeaway
The top tier (Binyan, The Boundary, plus mid-tier AU studios) still runs a classic DCC pipeline: CAD/BIM in, 3ds Max with V-Ray or Corona for stills, Unreal Engine for real-time sales suites, then post-production. AI shows up as a layer added to CGI base scenes (for example AI motion on CGI plates), not as a replacement. I found almost no public data on team sizes or formal AI disclosure from the named studios.

### Cited Findings
- Binyan Studios has offices in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, London, New York and Los Angeles. Its core pipeline is built on 3ds Max, worked into a process for narrative, storytelling visualisation. — [Autodesk AREA: From Sketch to Render, Binyan](https://area.autodesk.com/life-in-3d/from-sketch-to-render-using-3ds-max-binyan-studios/); [Digital Media World](https://www.digitalmediaworld.tv/vfx/binyan-uses-3ds-max-to-bring-drama-and-storytelling-to-archviz-projects); [CGconnect profile](https://cgconnect.chaos.com/companies/62429b7a-binyan-studios-3d-visualisation?from=cgarchitect)
- Binyan uses Adobe Substance 3D to build environments and materials (2021 case study). — [Adobe blog](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2021/10/25/how-binyan-creates-photoreal-architecture-in-3d)
- A Binyan senior 3D artist (LinkedIn, profile snippet, not an official case study) describes AI video work that starts from CGI base scenes and adds motion. The team directs story, camera, shots and transitions, then edits and post-produces, and the artist stresses it is "more than simply generating AI video". This is the clearest sign that a top AU studio uses AI motion on top of its own CGI. — [Search result via LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/louise-richardson/) (weak source: profile snippet)
- The Boundary (UK) used Chaos Corona as the exclusive pre-rendering engine for the full marketing suite of The Ariel at London's Television Centre. — [Chaos archviz page](https://www.chaos.com/architectural-visualization)
- The Boundary sells "Realspace", a real-time, interactive sales platform for unbuilt property, used on touchscreens or in VR. Buyers walk rooms, swap finishes, change lighting and judge scale. No engine, pricing or turnaround is published, and the page says nothing about AI. — [The Boundary: Realspace Realtime Interiors](https://www.the-boundary.com/work/realtime-interiors)
- A Perth developer using Realspace reported buyer meetings growing from 30 to 90 minutes (sponsored article). — [The Urban Developer: Beyond Renders](https://www.theurbandeveloper.com/articles/beyond-renders-immersive-digital-display-suites)
- A typical AU studio workflow, as described by an AU studio: CAD/BIM ingestion (Revit, ArchiCAD, AutoCAD, SketchUp), finish schedule mapping, clay proofs, then high-resolution rendering and grading in V-Ray, Corona or Unreal Engine 5, delivered at 4K and 8K. — [Elipse Studio, Sept 2026](https://elipsestudio.com/blog/architectural-visualisation-for-australian-property-developers)
- Feasly's 2026 developer guide names Binyan Studios, Studio Jam and Two o Seven as the established AU CGI studios, and reIMG as a "technology-led" studio that pairs accurate inputs with AI-assisted production and human QC. — [Feasly, 8 June 2026](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/architectural-rendering-australia-developer-guide)
- Industry adoption: a 2025 Chaos/Enscape survey found over 44% of respondents had started experimenting with AI tools. — [Enscape 2025 State of Archviz](https://blog.enscape3d.com/2025-state-of-archviz-report-insights)
- A US studio (Freedes) openly markets a hybrid pipeline: Midjourney, Kling AI and Gemini "Nano Banana" layered next to traditional render engines (press release, May 2026). — [IPS News press release](https://ipsnews.net/business/2026/05/19/freedes-studio-introduces-ai-enhanced-architectural-visualization-workflow-to-help-u-s-developers-sell-properties-faster/?amp=1)

### Inferences
- Incumbents are model-first (accurate geometry), with AI in post and motion. The defensible line for any new product is the same: geometry from the design model, AI only for dressing and motion.
- Real-time (Unreal) sales suites are a separate, expensive product line at the top studios (The Boundary Realspace, Elipse "digital twin").

### Gaps
- No public team sizes for Binyan, The Boundary or the others.
- Not researched or not found this session: Mir (Norway), Squint/Opera (UK), Doug and Wolf, Bloomimages (AU/DE), Pixelflakes (UK), Virtual Built, Archsense, Real Serious Games. General knowledge, unverified here: Mir is a high-end Norwegian stills studio; Squint/Opera is a London creative and real-time studio; Bloomimages has Hamburg and Sydney roots and sells interactive sales tools. Confirm on their own sites before citing.
- No studio formally disclosing AI use on its website was found.

## Typical prices in AUD and turnaround

### Takeaway
In AU, a marketing hero exterior costs about A$1,500 to A$6,000+, an interior still A$800 to A$5,000, a 60 s fly-through A$4,000 to A$25,000+, and a full off-the-plan campaign about A$14,000 to A$35,000. Real-time or VR suites run A$30,000 to A$60,000+. Turnaround runs from days for a single still to 4-6 weeks for a campaign and 6-10 weeks for interactive work. Offshore freelancers charge about US$20-40/hr.

### Cited Findings
- Feasly typical AU 2025-26 ranges: concept exterior A$300-2,500; marketing exterior hero A$1,500-6,000+; interior still A$800-5,000; aerial or streetscape A$1,800-10,000+; verified-view photomontage A$1,500-8,000+; 60 s fly-through A$4,000-25,000+; 360 panorama A$800-4,000; full VR sales suite A$5,000-40,000+. — [Feasly, June 2026](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/architectural-rendering-australia-developer-guide)
- Feasly: total render spend on a boutique apartment scheme runs from low tens of thousands to low hundreds of thousands of dollars, and marketing budgets are about 3-5% of GRV on apartment projects. Display suite fitouts cost a few hundred thousand for a modest prefab, up to several million for a flagship gallery. — [Feasly](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/architectural-rendering-australia-developer-guide)
- Feasly turnaround: single still, a few days; 5-image package, 2-3 weeks; animation, 4-6 weeks; verified photomontage, 6-10 weeks. — [Feasly](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/architectural-rendering-australia-developer-guide)
- Elipse Studio packages: boutique residential (1 exterior + 3 interior stills, 4K) A$4,500-9,000 in 2-3 weeks; full off-the-plan campaign (8-12 renders, drone photomontage, 60 s 4K walkthrough) A$14,000-28,000 in 4-6 weeks; interactive digital twin (UE5 walkthrough, browser 3D floor plan explorer, material toggles) A$30,000-60,000+ in 6-10 weeks. A CGI suite of A$12,000-35,000 compares with a physical display suite at A$250,000-500,000+ (12-20 weeks to build). — [Elipse Studio, Sept 2026](https://elipsestudio.com/blog/architectural-visualisation-for-australian-property-developers)
- AU per-image price lists: A$650-3,000 is the mainstream band per render, with A$100-250 as a "caution" budget tier; some AU studios list A$775-3,500 or A$800-3,500 per image. — [Search summary of AU studio pages, incl. Rendervision](https://rendervision.com.au/?p=1194); [Coohom AU guide](https://www.coohom.com/article/understanding-3d-rendering-prices-in-australia) (vendor/SEO pages, low confidence)
- Global animation benchmarks: a 60 s flythrough averages about US$2,966 (range US$1,000-16,000); US firms average US$6,120 and European firms US$3,950. Another guide puts per finished minute at US$2,000-15,000+. — [Advids flythrough cost](https://advids.co/pricing/how-much-animated-flythrough-video-creation-cost); [CADCrowd](https://www.cadcrowd.com/blog/3d-flythrough-3d-architectural-animations-costs-rates-and-pricing-for-companies/)
- Offshore rates: Upwork's 3D artist median is US$25-40/hr; archviz job posts list US$18-35/hr and US$20-40/hr; agencies about US$55/hr with US$1k minimums; specialists about US$75/hr. — [Upwork 3D artist cost](https://www.upwork.com/hire/3d-artists/cost/); [Upwork facade job](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Rendering-Facade-with-Close-Options_~021990550657398536163)
- Fiverr AU-based archviz animation gigs list roughly A$400-1,000 with 3-12 day delivery (scope and quality unclear). — [Search summary, Fiverr listing](https://www.fiverr.com/anabdullahdezyn/create-house-animation-architectural-exterior)

### Inferences
- An interactive apartment selector is usually bundled into a "digital twin" or sales-suite build (A$30k-60k+), not sold standalone at small-builder prices.
- Campaign prices are aimed at apartment towers where 3-5% of GRV is a large budget. A townhouse project of 6-20 dwellings has a much smaller budget, so A$14k-28k is a big ask there.

### Gaps
- No public price list from Binyan, The Boundary or other top-tier studios. Top-tier pricing likely sits above the Feasly and Elipse ranges, but I couldn't confirm it.
- No standalone AU price for an apartment selector widget.

## Who is using AI in production, how do they disclose it, and what worries clients?

### Takeaway
AI is used for motion on CGI plates, post-production, concept work and existing-home video, but not to replace model-accurate off-the-plan imagery. I found no studio disclosure standard. Client and regulator concerns centre on accuracy (geometry drift, invented features) and on "housefishing" (AI-altered listing photos).

### Cited Findings
- Maverick Frame (AU studio) says AI photo-to-video (Kling, Luma Dream Machine, Google Veo, Invideo, AutoReel, PropertyVideos.ai, VideoTour.ai) works for finished listings but fails for off-the-plan work: there is nothing accurate to photograph, it causes artifacts (bent door frames, drifting furniture, wrong reflections) and spec drift between runs, and it hurts trust at high price points. It gives no disclosure advice. — [Maverick Frame, 5 Nov 2026](https://maverickframe.com/blog/ai-video-for-real-estate/)
- Maverick Frame also claims fast AI concepts add towers, change street widths and drop buildings that are not in the plan (competitor claim). — [Search summary, Maverick Frame urban design rendering](https://maverickframe.com/solutions/urban-design-rendering/)
- Feasly: AI inside established design software (Veras, D5 Render) keeps geometry accurate. Purely generative tools (ArchiVinci, MyArchitectAI, Midjourney) suit concept work but can't guarantee dimensional accuracy. For 2026, traditional CGI is more reliable for approvals and commercial deliverables. — [Feasly](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/architectural-rendering-australia-developer-guide)
- The Urban Developer warns that as renders become more sophisticated, "potentially even enabled by artificial intelligence", transparency matters more. — [The Urban Developer: Render v reality (search snippet)](https://www.theurbandeveloper.com/articles/render-v-reality-avoiding-legal-quagmires) (could not fetch the full text)
- The ACCC says businesses using AI to generate advertising should check it for false, misleading or inaccurate content. Property-specific rules sit with the states. — [Money Magazine](https://www.moneymag.com.au/ai-edited-real-estate-photos-misleading-buyers)
- CHOICE documented "housefishing": buyers and renters calling out agents' AI-altered images. — [CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/money/property/articles/housefishing-warning-house-hunters-call-out-real-estate-agents-using-ai-images)
- A Melbourne apartment campaign got wide attention for AI-generated exotic animals in its imagery. — [Real Estate Business](https://www.realestatebusiness.com.au/tech/32045-the-unusual-ai-marketing-technique-turning-heads-and-doubling-sales)
- Clients now rework architects' renders with AI themselves before meetings (Canada). — [LearnArchitecture](https://learnarchitecture.net/artificial-intelligence/42339-clients-editing-architects-renderings-with-ai.html)
- From 1 July 2026, Domain offers 3D capture and photoreal visualisation to vendors in Melbourne and other cities. — [Search summary, B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/domain-uses-drones-to-help-reshape-australias-property-information-infrastructure/)

### Inferences
- The client's main worry is legal exposure from features that drift away from the approved plan. A pipeline that can prove every frame comes from the approved model is a selling point, not just an internal process.
- Disclosure is not yet standard. A clear label system (for example "geometry from approved model; landscaping and furniture indicative; AI-assisted finishing") would set a new product apart.

### Gaps
- No public AI disclosure policy from Binyan, The Boundary, Squint/Opera, Mir or the others.
- No survey of AU developer attitudes to AI renders.

## Productised and automated competitors (SaaS sales tools, AI staging)

### Takeaway
The productised layer is mostly sales-presentation software (apartment selectors, digital display suites, sales CRM) that still needs a studio to make the 3D assets. AI virtual staging is cheap (about US$0.25-6 per image) but only works on photos of existing rooms. I found no product that takes a design model and turns out an accurate staged scroll-story website.

### Cited Findings
- Volume Vision (AU): an enterprise sales suite (exterior unit selection with availability, interior and amenity walkthroughs, render gallery), plus a self-serve version for "rendering studios, sales agencies and brokers" with no credit card and cancel anytime. No pricing is public, and the self-serve page is on a preview domain. — [The Urban Developer (sponsored)](https://www.theurbandeveloper.com/articles/from-showroom-to-smartphone-volume-vision-s-all-access-sales-suite); [Volume Vision self-serve preview](https://offplan-preview.pages.dev/landing-v1)
- Vision Pitch (AU): off-the-plan sales tools including floor selectors, 360 drone views and analytics. — [Vision Pitch](https://visionpitch.com.au/?p=3110)
- Maverick Frame and Render Vision sell interactive apartment selectors (building to floor to unit to finishes, live price and availability) as studio services. — [Maverick Frame selector](https://maverickframe.com/services/interactive-apartment-selector/); [Render Vision viewer](https://render-vision.com/services/3d-apartment-viewer-services/)
- Spec3D (with Umbraco) is pitched as replacing static floorplans for off-plan sales. — [Kayo Digital](https://www.kayo.digital/kayo-how/the-end-of-the-static-floorplan-how-spec3d-and-umbraco-are-transforming-off-plan-property-sales/)
- WIZIO Verse: cloud SaaS for off-plan sales (no prices). — [Capterra AU](https://www.capterra.com.au/software/1108967/WIZIO-Verse)
- Unlatch (France): digitises residential sales for developers and brokers. Reported 400 clients and €11.7bn GMV (April 2025), with a conflicting source saying about 250 clients. Series A 2020, about US$6.7m raised, expanding to the UK, Spain, Portugal and Benelux. No AU presence found. — [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/directory/unlatch/); [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/getunlatch)
- VividWorks (Finland): a 3D configurator for modular products including homes, from about €447-847/month (Capterra) or €647/US$697/month (own site). — [Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/1014079/vividworks); [VividWorks pricing](https://www.vividworks.com/pricing-new)
- AI virtual staging prices (affiliate and blog sources, conflicting): Collov about US$19/month for 60 images (about US$0.32 per image, down to about US$0.23 at volume); REimagineHome US$19/month for 30 images up to US$119/month for 1,200; Virtual Staging AI about US$1-6 per image or US$16/month for 6 photos. — [aihomedesign.com](https://aihomedesign.com/blog/services/virtual-staging-software); [fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/best-virtual-staging-ai-2026/)
- Matterport's "Project Genesis" adds AI renovation visualisation to scanned 3D homes. — [Elite Agent](https://eliteagent.com/from-vision-to-reality-how-matterports-ai-innovations-are-reshaping-property)

### Inferences
- The market splits in two: studios make the assets (expensive and slow), and platforms present them (subscriptions). Nobody owns automated model-to-assets-to-site for small projects.
- AI staging sets a price anchor of a few dollars an image, but it can't do off-the-plan work. That leaves an opening for "AI staging, but driven by the model".

### Gaps
- Not found or not researched: Pixelflakes, Archistar (AU, feasibility and design AI), Zuru (AU/NZ builder), Bloomimages interactive, Real Serious Games. Their pricing and model-to-marketing features are unconfirmed.
- No public pricing for Volume Vision, Vision Pitch or Realspace.

## Legal and marketing rules in Australia for artist's impressions

### Takeaway
ACL s18 (misleading or deceptive conduct) is the main risk. Ripani v Century Legend shows an "artist's impression" label does not cure a render that shows something unbuildable. NSW has added rules for off-the-plan contracts and, from early 2027, AI-image disclosure for rental ads. I didn't find an AI-specific rule for sales renders.

### Cited Findings
- Ripani v Century Legend Pty Ltd [2022] FCA 242 (judgment 18 March 2022): the buyers paid A$9.58m off the plan for apartment 14.01 at The Victoriana, Melbourne. The hero render showed a free-span opening and a flush threshold to the terrace. Before the contract, the developer's architects had advised that this could not be built and recommended the "artist's impression" label. The court rescinded the contract and awarded A$380,000. The label did not cure the image, because renders are a "proxy for an inspection" and images convey meaning holistically. A brochure disclaimer on page 96 in small, low-contrast type was "inutile". The sales agent was not liable because it was unaware of the architects' warnings. — [REIQ, 5 Apr 2022](https://www.reiq.com/articles/property-sales/developer-liable-for-misleading-and-deceptive-hero-render-in-off-the-plan-sale)
- The real opening was about 3.4 m. Feasly says the result was upheld on appeal. — [Feasly](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/architectural-rendering-australia-developer-guide); contradicted in part by [Maddocks](https://www.maddocks.com.au/insights/off-the-plan-sales-misleading-deceptive-conduct) and search summaries saying the Full Court (Century Legend v Ripani [2022] FCAFC 191) ordered a new trial limited to whether the buyers kept relying on the conduct. Check the FCAFC judgment.
- Recommended controls: architect sign-off on every render, conservative depiction of uncertain features, render-specific disclaimers, and contracts that reference plans and finishes schedules rather than images. — [Feasly](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/architectural-rendering-australia-developer-guide)
- Reported ACL penalties: up to A$220,000 for individuals and A$1.1m for companies. — [Money Magazine](https://www.moneymag.com.au/ai-edited-real-estate-photos-misleading-buyers) (this is the older penalty tier; current ACL maximums for companies are much higher; verify)
- NSW off-the-plan regime (since 2019): a disclosure statement must be attached (including sunset dates), the sunset clause definition is widened, and cooling-off is 10 business days. — [NSW Registrar General](https://www.registrargeneral.nsw.gov.au/property-and-conveyancing/off-the-plan); [NSW regulation](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/pdf/inforce/2025-11-10/sl-2022-0485)
- NSW AI-image law (passed Sept 2026): landlords and agents must disclose digitally generated or altered images "reasonably likely to mislead or deceive" in rental ads. Penalty up to A$22,000, in force early 2027. It does not cover sales, but altered sales photos are already barred under existing law and the ACL. It does not mention artist impressions of unbuilt property. — [SmartCompany, 29 Sept 2026](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/property/realty-check-nsw-demands-disclosures-for-ai-altered-property-photos/)
- Victoria: false or misleading representations about a property's characteristics are unlawful (Consumer Affairs Victoria). — [Money Magazine](https://www.moneymag.com.au/ai-edited-real-estate-photos-misleading-buyers)
- Planning-grade imagery follows separate rules: NSW visual impact principles (Tenacity, Rose Bay), VCAT Practice Note PNVCAT2, and the City of Perth photomontage requirement. — [Feasly](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/architectural-rendering-australia-developer-guide)

### Inferences
- A model-driven pipeline that logs which plan revision each frame came from, and flags indicative elements on screen (not on page 96), directly answers Ripani. That's a sellable compliance feature.
- Stage visuals (cutaway, frame, finished) are lower risk if they're shown as construction sequence. Finishes and views should carry visible, frame-level disclaimers.

### Gaps
- Not found: Victorian (Sale of Land Act s9AA and related) and Queensland (Land Sales Act / Body Corporate) off-the-plan disclosure specifics. Not covered this session.
- No ACCC guidance specific to artist's impressions or AI renders.
- Ripani appeal outcome: sources conflict.

## Where the gap is for a small studio with an automated pipeline

### Takeaway
There's a clear gap between A$100-500 offshore or AI images (inaccurate, no story) and A$14k-60k studio campaigns and sales suites (accurate, slow, built for towers). A model-driven scroll showcase priced around A$3k-10k and delivered in days would suit townhouse developers, small builders and boutique projects. Its case rests on accuracy (traceable to the approved model) and speed, not on photoreal hero quality.

### Cited Findings
- Studio campaigns cost A$14,000-28,000 over 4-6 weeks, and interactive builds A$30,000-60,000+ over 6-10 weeks. — [Elipse Studio](https://elipsestudio.com/blog/architectural-visualisation-for-australian-property-developers)
- Boutique residential still packages cost A$4,500-9,000 over 2-3 weeks. — [Elipse Studio](https://elipsestudio.com/blog/architectural-visualisation-for-australian-property-developers)
- Multi-image packages get cheaper per image once a model exists. — [Feasly](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/architectural-rendering-australia-developer-guide)
- Generic AI video tools fail on off-the-plan accuracy and spec consistency. — [Maverick Frame](https://maverickframe.com/blog/ai-video-for-real-estate/)
- Platform vendors such as Volume Vision are already moving down-market with self-serve for studios and brokers. — [Volume Vision self-serve preview](https://offplan-preview.pages.dev/landing-v1)
- Static hero renders leave buyers guessing about the unbuilt parts, and interactive suites lengthen sales meetings. — [The Urban Developer: Beyond Renders](https://www.theurbandeveloper.com/articles/beyond-renders-immersive-digital-display-suites)

### Inferences
- **Price point:** sit between the A$4.5k-9k boutique still package and the A$14k-28k campaign. Something like A$3k-10k for a scroll site plus a set of stills and frames could work. Then charge a monthly fee to host and update the site, mirroring platform subscriptions.
- **Speed:** the incumbents' bottleneck is per-project modelling and art direction. If the input is the architect's own model (IFC, Revit, SketchUp, Rhino), turnaround can shrink to days, against weeks.
- **Product angle:** the stage sequence (exterior to cutaway to frame to finished) is not a standard incumbent deliverable. It suits builders, who sell build quality, and it reuses one model across all stages.
- **Trust angle:** "every frame from the approved model, with indicative elements labelled" answers both Ripani and the AI-accuracy concerns that incumbents raise against generative tools.
- **Risks:** top studios can add AI motion to their own CGI (Binyan already does). Platforms (Volume Vision) could add automated asset generation. Hero-image quality will lag top-tier CGI, so position it as a sales explainer, not a hero campaign.

### Gaps
- No data found on how many AU townhouse or small-builder projects buy visuals, or what they spend.
- No AU competitor found that offers an automated model-to-scroll-website product. That's an absence, not proof none exist.
